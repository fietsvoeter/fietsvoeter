Na jaren trial and error met verschillende setups van hardtail forks tot full suspension enduro bikes deel ik exacte procedures voor elke rider om optimale demping te vinden.

Het fundamentele doel van vering is wielen contact met grond behouden terwijl impact krachten absorberen. Te zachte demping laat fiets dobberen. Te harde demping laat wielen stuiten over obstakels. Sweet spot ertussen geeft traction en controle. Voor algemene [MTB beginner tips](/blog/mtb-voor-beginners/) en uitrusting keuzes kun je onze andere gidsen bekijken.

## Demping Basics En Terminologie

Begrijpen hoe vering werkt helpt logische setup beslissingen maken. Spring en damper werken samen om wiel beweging te controleren. Spring absorbeert impact, damper controleert snelheid van beweging. Beide moeten correct afgesteld voor gebalanceerde performance.

Spring system kan coil of air zijn. Coil springs zijn stalen veren met lineaire kracht curve. Air springs gebruiken luchtdruk met progressive curve. Coil geeft consistent feel, air is lichter en verstelbaar. Voor- en nadelen van beide systemen zijn onderwerp voor ander artikel. Deze gids focust op demping niet spring type.

Sag is hoeveelheid travel gebruikt onder rider gewicht stationary. Uitgedrukt als percentage van total travel. Bijvoorbeeld 30mm sag op 150mm fork is 20 procent. Sag bepaalt operating point van vering. Te weinig sag betekent vering werkt alleen bij grote hits. Te veel sag betekent vering constant aan einde travel tijdens normale riding.

Rebound damping controleert snelheid waarmee vering extend na compressie. Te slow rebound en vering blijft ingedrukt na bump pakking volgende bump met less travel beschikbaar. Te fast rebound en fiets springt terug throwing rider around. Correcte rebound allows vering om recover tussen bumps without bouncing.

Compression damping controleert snelheid waarmee vering compress tijdens impact. Low-speed compression affects body movements zoals pedaling and braking. High-speed compression affects hitting bumps and drops. Don't confuse speed terms with bike speed. Speed refers to shaft velocity not how fast you're riding.

Lockout is maximum compression damping functie. Eliminates vering movement during climbs. Useful on smooth fireroads, detrimental on technical terrain. Most riders overuse lockout when proper compression setting would be better. Lockout is convenience feature not substitute for proper setup.

## Sag Instellen Eerste Stap

Sag is foundation van alle andere settings. Get this right first voor everything else to work properly. Incorrect sag makes dialing rebound and compression impossible. Most riders skip this step or do it wrong. Don't be lazy here.

Target sag depends on discipline. XC racing uses 15 tot 20 procent voor efficiency. Trail riding uses 20 tot 25 procent voor balance. Enduro uses 25 tot 30 procent voor plushness. Downhill uses 30 tot 35 procent voor maximum absorption. More sag gives better small bump compliance but reduces support for big hits.

Measuring sag requires o-ring on fork stanchion and shock shaft. Slide o-ring against seal. Sit on bike in normal riding position without bouncing. Carefully dismount without compressing suspension further. Measure distance o-ring moved. This is sag amount. Divide by total travel times 100 for percentage.

Adding air pressure increases preload reducing sag. Most forks have valve on top of right leg. Use **RockShox** or **Fox** high volume shock pump. Never use floor pump or compressor. Pressure range is typically 50 tot 150 PSI depending on rider weight and desired sag. Start conservative around manufacturer recommendation.

Setup pressure incrementally. Add 5 PSI at time, test ride, measure sag again. Don't add large amounts chasing number. Suspension feels different between static sag measurement and dynamic riding. Target sag in riding position is starting point, actual riding feel determines final pressure.

Rider weight affects required pressure significantly. **Fox** and **RockShox** publish pressure charts based on rider weight and desired sag. These are starting points not gospel. Heavier riders need more pressure. Riders carrying backpack need additional 5 tot 10 PSI accounting for load. Test with gear you actually ride with.

## Rebound Demping Afstellen

Rebound determines hoe fast vering returns after compression. Too slow and vering packs down. Too fast and bike bounces. Correct rebound depends on sag, rider weight, and terrain. Most riders run rebound too slow causing packed suspension.

Starting point is full-speed rebound middle of adjustment range. Most forks and shocks have red or blue adjuster marked with plus and minus. Plus slows rebound minus speeds it up. From middle position, test ride technical section with repetitive bumps. Feel if vering is recovering between hits.

Test rebound with curb drop method. Roll off curb at walking speed. Bike should compress then extend once smoothly. If bike bounces or pogos, rebound is too fast. If bike stays compressed taking multiple bounces to extend, rebound is too slow. Adjust one click at time until single smooth extension occurs.

Front and rear rebound should be balanced. General rule is rear rebound slightly slower than front. This prevents bike from pivoting over front wheel during rough descents. Exact balance depends on bike geometry and rider preference. Some aggressive riders prefer faster rear for playful feel.

Heavy riders need slower rebound settings. More mass means more momentum during extension requiring stronger damping to control. Light riders need faster rebound to ensure vering recovers quickly. Body weight is dominant factor, ignore rebound settings other riders use unless they weigh similar to you.

Terrain affects optimal rebound. Smooth fast trails allow faster rebound for responsive feel. Rocky technical trails need slower rebound preventing harsh extension after big hits. If riding varied terrain, compromise toward slower side. Too slow is rideable, too fast is harsh and uncontrolled.

Counter-intuitive principle is slower rebound often feels faster. When rebound is too fast, rider subconsciously braces against bouncing slowing overall pace. Proper rebound allows relaxed riding increasing confidence and speed. Don't chase fastest setting, chase controlled confident feeling.

## Compressie Demping Instellen

Compression damping prevents vering from blowing through travel too easily. Low-speed compression affects pedaling efficiency and body movements. High-speed compression affects impact absorption. Modern forks and shocks separate these adjustments allowing independent tuning.

Low-speed compression starting point is quarter turn from fully open. This provides support during pedaling without harshness during trail riding. If bike wallows or bobs excessively during climbing, add compression. If bike feels harsh on small bumps, reduce compression. Changes affect overall suppleness significantly.

High-speed compression affects big hits and square edge bumps. Too little and vering bottoms harshly. Too much and small bump sensitivity suffers. Most riders should leave high-speed compression at middle setting. Only advanced riders needing specific tuning should adjust this. Getting it wrong makes bike worse not better.

Testing compression requires aggressive riding. Pedal hard standing up on smooth trail. If bike bobs or squats excessively, add low-speed compression. Then ride rough section with roots and rocks. If bike feels harsh or skips over bumps, reduce compression. Balance between efficiency and compliance is goal.

Volume spacers affect end-stroke progression independent of compression damping. Adding spacers makes last portion of travel more resistant preventing harsh bottom-outs. **RockShox** calls these tokens, **Fox** calls them volume spacers. Same function different name. For riders frequently bottoming, add spacer before maxing compression damping.

Platform compression is marketing term for firm low-speed compression setting. Provides pedaling support without full lockout. Useful for fire roads and smooth climbing. Don't confuse with proper compression damping adjustment. Platform is convenience setting not replacement for correct baseline compression tune.

## Fork Specifieke Setup Procedures

Fork setup follows same principles but has specific considerations. Negative spring, oil levels, and stanchion maintenance all affect performance. Proper fork maintenance is prerequisite for consistent damping behavior. Dirty seals and low oil cause unpredictable damping.

Negative spring or negative air chamber creates initial plushness. Some forks require negative pressure set separately from positive. **RockShox** Pike and Lyrik have this feature. Add negative pressure for supple off-top feel. Too much negative and fork rides too deep in travel. Start with equal positive and negative adjusting from there.

Oil height affects ramp-up at end of stroke. Lower oil creates progressive spring rate preventing harsh bottom-outs. Higher oil softens end stroke for plush feel. This adjustment requires fork disassembly and precise measurement. For most riders, factory oil height is adequate. Only suspension tuners should modify this.

Stanchion cleaning prevents seal damage and contamination. After every ride wipe stanchions with clean cloth. Weekly apply suspension specific lubricant like **RockShox** Gold or **Fox** Gold. This reduces friction and prevents seal drying. Contaminated seals cause binding and inconsistent damping feel.

Lower leg service every 50 hours replaces bath oil lubricating bushings. This is critical maintenance often neglected. Worn bushings cause slop and binding. Service interval seems short but MTB riding is demanding. Cheaper to maintain regularly than repair damaged stanchions from bushing wear.

Fork pressure changes with temperature and altitude. Air expands when warm reducing pressure. Altitude reduces atmospheric pressure affecting fork behavior. For rides with significant elevation change or temperature swings, check pressure at trailhead. 5 PSI difference is noticeable in feel.

## Shock Setup Voor Full Suspension

Rear shock setup is more complex than fork due to linkage leverage ratios. Understanding linkage behavior helps make informed shock setup decisions. Shock settings interact with frame design in ways fork settings don't.

Linkage leverage ratio determines shock force relative to wheel force. Progressive linkage multiplies force at end of travel. Regressive linkage has opposite effect. Most modern bikes use progressive linkages. This affects required shock pressure and compression damping. Same shock on different frames needs different setup.

Shock eyelet bushings and hardware must be free. Binding hardware causes harsh feel mimicking excessive compression damping. Remove shock, operate by hand. Should move smoothly through entire stroke. If sticky, service bushings and check frame pivot bearings. Suspension tuning won't fix binding hardware.

Air can volume spacers are essential for preventing harsh bottom-outs on aggressive bikes. Most shocks come with zero or one spacer. Adding spacers increases progression making last 20mm of travel much firmer. For riders using full travel regularly, add spacer and retest. Experimenting with spacer count is free tuning.

Metric sizing confusion causes setup problems. Older imperial shocks measured stroke in inches. Modern metric shocks measure in millimeters. 50mm stroke is roughly 2 inch. Ensure replacement shock matches frame requirements. Incorrect stroke length changes geometry and suspension characteristics dramatically.

Remote lockout adds convenience maar increases complexity. Cable stretch and contamination affect performance. Maintain remote lockout diligently or accept unpredictable behavior. Some riders remove remote accepting inconvenience of reaching shock lockout lever. Eliminates one more thing to break on trail.

## Hardtail Specific Overwegingen

Hardtail setup is simpler without rear shock maar fork becomes more critical. All rear end compliance comes from tire and rider body. Fork setup determines overall bike character more than on full suspension bike.

Hardtail riders benefit from slightly more sag than full suspension equivalent. 25 tot 30 procent fork sag gives better traction compensating for rigid rear end. Too little sag and rear tire skips over bumps. More fork sag allows front to drop into holes maintaining rear contact.

Tire pressure is rear suspension on hardtail. Lower pressures increase compliance and traction. Experiment with 0.2 bar increments finding minimum pressure without pinch flats. Wider tires allow lower pressures. Voor Nederlandse trails with roots, 1.6 tot 1.8 bar in 2.4 inch tire works well.

Hardtail fork should be plusher than full suspension fork. Run rebound slightly faster and compression slightly softer. This maximizes traction from front tire which is doing extra work compensating for rigid rear. Goal is balanced feel where both wheels track terrain similarly.

Seatpost insertion affects rear compliance. More seatpost exposed increases flex absorbing bumps. Voor hardtail, max seatpost height is more comfortable than minimum insertion. Dropper post reduces compliance when lowered eliminating flex. Accept this trade-off for descending capability dropper provides.

Frame material affects setup preferences. Steel and titanium frames flex more than aluminum and carbon. Flexible frames need less plush suspension. Stiff frames benefit from suppler fork settings. Test different settings learning how your specific frame behaves. Geometry and material both matter.

## Setup Voor Verschillende Terrein Types

Optimal suspension setup varies with terrain. Veluwe roots require different settings than Ardennen rocks. Rather than searching for universal perfect setup, optimize for terrain you ride most frequently. Accept compromise when riding unfamiliar terrain.

Root heavy trails benefit from supple compression and fast rebound. Roots are countless small hits requiring vering to recover rapidly. Add sag for better tracking, open compression for compliance. Fast rebound ensures fork extends between hits. This setup feels too soft on smooth trails maar excels on roots.

Rocky technical terrain needs more bottom-out resistance. Add compression damping and volume spacers preventing harsh impacts on large rocks. Slower rebound prevents fork extending too quickly after big hits throwing you forward. This setup feels overdamped on smooth trails maar provides control on rocks.

Flow trails with jumps require balanced setup. Moderate sag provides support for takeoffs. Medium rebound prevents pogo on landings. Moderate compression allows full travel usage without harshness. This all-around setup works adequately everywhere excelling nowhere. Voor bike parks with varied features, balanced approach is safest.

Muddy conditions need slightly firmer setup. Mud clogs seals reducing performance. Extra compression damping compensates for contaminated seals. More volume spacers prevent mud from robbing travel. After muddy ride, clean stanchions immediately preventing seal damage. Setup changes are temporary muddy day accommodation.

Altitude affects air pressure significantly. Gain 1000 meter elevation and suspension pressure drops 10 percent. Add air at high altitude trailheads. Alternatively use volume spacers compensating for pressure loss with progression. For riders frequently riding mountains, carry shock pump adjusting pressure per location.

## Troubleshooting Veelvoorkomende Problemen

Weird suspension behavior often stems from maintenance issues not setup problems. Before endless adjustments, eliminate mechanical problems. Binding hardware and contaminated seals ruin best setup. Mechanical soundness is prerequisite for tuning.

Fork feels harsh on all settings. Possible causes are contaminated seals, low bath oil, or bound bushings. Service lower legs replacing oil and cleaning seals. Check stanchions for scratches. Worn bushings require complete overhaul. Don't waste time adjusting settings if mechanical problem exists.

Shock bottoms harshly even with maximum pressure. Add volume spacers increasing progression. If already maximum spacers, shock is wrong size for frame or rider is too heavy. Some riders need custom spring rates. Budget solution is add even more spacers using aftermarket options allowing more than factory maximum.

Inconsistent damping feel suggests air mixing with oil. Requires shock servicing separating air and oil chambers properly. Cannot be fixed by user adjustment. Ship to suspension service specialist. Inconsistent damping ruins riding experience and confidence. Don't ignore this problem.

Fork leaking oil from seals needs immediate attention. Continued riding damages stanchions permanently. New seals are cheap fix. Damaged stanchions require expensive replacement. Service leaking fork immediately don't procrastinate. Oil dripping onto brakes causes contamination ruining brake pads and rotors.

Suspension feels different after temperature change. Air pressure changes with temperature. Check pressure when conditions change significantly. 10 degrees temperature swing causes noticeable pressure difference. Summer to winter pressure requirements differ by 10 tot 15 PSI. Recheck settings seasonally.

Creaking sounds from suspension are usually frame bearings not damper. Check all pivot bearings for play and dirt. Properly maintained bearings are silent. Rebuild bearings annually or more if riding in wet conditions. Creaking front end is often headset not fork. Isolate noise source before servicing wrong component.
